Plutus privacy policy

Last updated 28 October 2026.

Plutus is a personal, read-only spending tracker for Android, built and used by its developer for their own bank account. It has no server, no user accounts, no analytics and no advertising.

What Plutus reads

With your consent, given at your bank, Plutus reads the balance and transactions of the account you connect: amounts, dates, counterparty names and IBANs, and descriptions. It reads them through Enable Banking, a licensed account information service. Plutus cannot make payments or change anything at your bank.

Where your data is kept

Only on your phone. Transactions are stored in an encrypted database (SQLCipher). Its key, your session details and your settings are encrypted with keys held in the Android Keystore. The app excludes itself from Android backups, so none of this is copied to the cloud. Plutus never sends your transactions anywhere.

Who else handles it

  • Your bank, which provides the data.
  • Enable Banking, which passes it from your bank to the app under its own privacy policy and terms.
  • GitHub Pages hosts the page your bank redirects to after you log in. That page stores nothing, loads nothing from elsewhere and sends no referrer, but GitHub may log your IP address as it does for any website.

How long it is kept

Until you remove it. Disconnect in the app revokes the bank access at Enable Banking. Uninstalling the app deletes all data and keys from your phone. Bank access also ends on its own when the consent you gave expires.
